A/B Testing Plan – Iowa City College Student Guide
Website: Iowa City College Student Guide
Test Page: Homepage
Experiment: Homepage Headline Comparison
1. What I Am Testing
I will test whether changing the main headline on my homepage makes visitors more interested in exploring my website.
Version A (Original): "Affordable student life in Iowa City"
Version B (New): "Make the Most of College Life in Iowa City"
The headline will be the only element changed between the two versions.
2. Hypothesis
I predict that Version B will encourage more visitors to explore my website because it focuses on the benefits of college life rather than just affordability. I believe this wording will appeal to a broader group of college students.
3. AI Visitors and Testing Method
I will use GPT-6 with the same settings for every trial to simulate college students in Iowa City. Each trial will take place in a new conversation without memory of previous trials.
Persona: A college student in Iowa City looking for affordable food, fitness, transportation, and activities.
Testing prompt:
"You are a college student in Iowa City looking for affordable food, fitness, transportation, and activities. Read the following homepage content. Would you click through to another page on this website? Answer YES or NO, followed by one sentence explaining why.
Homepage content:
[Insert complete Version A or Version B homepage content here.]" I will publish Version B as a separate page with a noindex tag and exclude it from my sitemap so it does not compete with Version A in search results.
I will create a randomized list of 100 assignments before testing, with exactly 50 assigned to Version A and 50 assigned to Version B. I will follow that order when running the trials.
4. Outcome and Sample Size
My primary outcome is the percentage of AI visitors who answer YES.
I will complete 100 trials total, with 50 for each version. I will collect all results before analyzing the data.
Planned launch: November 17, 2026
Expected completion: November 24, 2026
5. Comparing Results
I will compare the YES response percentages for Versions A and B and use a two-proportion statistical test to determine whether the difference could be due to chance.
My hypothesis will be supported if Version B receives a higher percentage of YES responses. I will also report whether the difference is statistically significant at the 5% level.
